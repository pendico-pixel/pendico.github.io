03 — Indian Legal System

Understanding Law in India

India follows a constitutional democratic system in which the Constitution is the supreme law and legislative, executive and judicial institutions operate within constitutional boundaries.

01

Constitution of India

The Constitution establishes the structure of government, distribution of powers, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and constitutional institutions.

02

Legislature

Parliament and State Legislatures enact statutes that regulate public and private affairs within their constitutional legislative competence.

03

Executive

The Union and State executives implement laws, administer public institutions and exercise executive powers subject to constitutional and statutory limitations.

04

Judiciary

Courts interpret legislation, resolve disputes, protect constitutional rights and exercise judicial review within the constitutional framework.

05

Fundamental Rights

Part III of the Constitution guarantees fundamental rights and provides important safeguards against unconstitutional State action.

06

Judicial Review

Indian constitutional courts can examine legislative and executive action for consistency with the Constitution.

04 — Courts

The Indian Judicial System

India has an integrated judicial structure with the Supreme Court at the apex, followed by High Courts and subordinate courts.

🏛 Supreme Court of India

Apex constitutional court of India with jurisdiction including constitutional, appellate and other matters provided by law.

⚖️ High Courts

Constitutional courts exercising jurisdiction over States and Union Territories, including writ, appellate and supervisory jurisdiction.

🏛 District & Subordinate Courts

Courts dealing with a large volume of civil, criminal and other trial-level and appellate proceedings under the applicable laws.

📜 Tribunals & Specialised Forums

Specialised adjudicatory bodies established under legislation to deal with particular categories of disputes and regulatory matters.

05 — Legal Profession

A Career in Law

Law is more than a degree. It is a profession built on knowledge, reasoning, ethics, drafting, advocacy and continuous learning.

⚖️ Advocacy

Advocates represent clients before courts and tribunals, prepare pleadings, conduct legal research, develop arguments and assist courts in the administration of justice.

🏛 Judiciary

Judicial service examinations provide a pathway to the judicial service of the State, subject to the applicable eligibility and recruitment rules.

🏢 Corporate Law

Corporate lawyers work on contracts, mergers, compliance, corporate governance, transactions, employment matters and regulatory issues.

🔎 Legal Research

Research-oriented careers involve analysing legislation, judgments, regulations, policy developments and complex legal questions.

🏛 Public Service

Legal professionals can pursue opportunities in government departments, public-sector institutions, commissions and statutory bodies.

🎓 Legal Academia

Teaching, scholarship and legal research offer another path for those interested in developing legal theory and contributing to legal education.
